02 / THE BEGINNING PERSONAL OBSERVATION

Before researching
the system, I encountered
one small problem.

My family has a connection with agriculture. During one onion crop, the produce was sold through a broker at a price that was below the retail rates visible in Bengaluru.

The experience raised a question that was much bigger than the price of one crop.

If the farmer could not easily see the wider market situation, and if reaching a direct buyer was difficult, where exactly was the information being lost?

That question became the starting point for deeper research into the agricultural ecosystem.

A single onion transaction made me look beyond the transaction itself. The real subject became the system.

THE QUESTION Where does value disappear between the farm and the market? This became one of the questions that shaped the research direction.
03 / OBSERVATION

The deeper I looked,
the more connected everything became.

What initially looked like a market-access problem gradually revealed relationships between people, movement, machines, information and decisions.

01 / FARM

The farmer

At the centre was the farmer — producing, deciding and carrying the consequences of information that may or may not reach them at the right moment.

02 / PEOPLE & MACHINES

Skills & resources

Labour, equipment and machine operators form another layer. Their availability, skills and coordination influence what can actually happen on the farm.

03 / MOVEMENT

Logistics

Once produce moves beyond the farm, transportation and coordination become part of the same story. Drivers and logistics providers connect physical movement with time.

04 / MARKET

The buyer

On the other side sits demand. Buyers need access to suitable produce, while farmers need a clearer path towards real demand.

These were never separate stories.

09 / RESEARCH EXPERIENCE

Research became a cycle
of looking deeper.

The project evolved through repeated observation, questioning, connection, design and improvement.

01 / LOOK

Start with what is visible.

The initial observation was simple: an agricultural transaction where the farmer did not have the same visibility into the wider market.

02 / LISTEN

Understand the people around it.

The research expanded towards the different people and resources that influence agricultural activity.

03 / CONNECT

Map the relationships.

Farmers, buyers, labour, equipment, operators, logistics and drivers began to appear as connected parts of one larger system.

04 / REIMAGINE

Question the original assumptions.

Instead of building around one isolated marketplace problem, the concept was reimagined around an interconnected ecosystem.

05 / DESIGN

Turn research into architecture.

The research informed the structure of roles, public information, identity, portals and the wider ecosystem.

06 / BUILD

Build, review and improve.

The architecture continued to evolve through repeated review and redesign rather than being treated as finished after the first version.

Research became Design , which became Review , which became Improvement.
